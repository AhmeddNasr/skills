# Practice Governance

External standards say what good practice looks like; they don't say which
artifacts *your* team must produce, who approves them, or how quality is judged.
This skill is the internal playbook that turns standards into consistent daily
practice — and it owns the **traceability chain**, the backbone every other skill
links into.

It governs *how the work runs*; it does not do the analysis. (That maps to BABOK's
Planning & Monitoring: the wrapper around everything else.)

---

## Where this sits

- **Wraps** every phase skill: each one registers its artifacts, owners,
  approvers, and quality rubric here.
- **Pairs with** route-to-knowledge skills — they route a task to the right
  knowledge area; this one says who owns the output and what gate it must pass.
- **Upstream:** strategy defines the initiatives that the lifecycle governs.

---

## Core working rules

### 1. Run an artifact catalogue
Every deliverable is classified **mandatory / conditional / optional** by initiative
type, and each carries an **owner role**, an **approver**, and a **quality rubric**.
An artifact with no owner or no rubric is ungoverned — fix before it's produced.

### 2. Manage competency by evidence, not quizzes
Four proficiency levels per capability — **Foundation, Practitioner, Advanced,
Lead**. Assess on a weighted basis: ~25% knowledge of standards, ~40% quality of
produced artifacts, ~25% application in a real business case, ~10% facilitation and
stakeholder outcomes. Knowledge tests alone do not certify a level.

### 3. Define quality gates between phases
Each lifecycle phase ends at a gate with explicit pass criteria. Nothing crosses a
gate without its mandatory artifacts at the required quality. Gates without written
criteria are theatre.

### 4. Fix approval authorities early
Name who signs off requirements, model changes, and scope decisions before work
starts. Late ambiguity here is the most common cause of stalled approvals and
rework (the Planning & Monitoring lesson).

### 5. Govern the repository
Naming conventions, versioning, ownership, and inter-artifact relationships are
defined and enforced. Models are governed assets with a single source of truth.

### 6. Own the traceability chain end to end
Maintain, for every initiative, the walkable chain: **strategic objective →
business outcome → capability/value stream → stakeholder need → business concept/
rule → process/decision → business requirement → system requirement → design
component → test case → operational KPI/benefit.** A broken link is a defect, not a
documentation gap.

## Mandatory outputs

- Role definitions and competency matrix (by role and level)
- Proficiency levels and training pathways
- Artifact catalogue (mandatory / conditional / optional)
- Modelling conventions (approved BPMN, UML, DMN, taxonomy, rule styles)
- Quality-review checklists and rubrics
- Approval authorities register
- Repository, naming, and version-control rules
- Lifecycle method with quality gates
- Traceability model and assessment/certification process

---

## Standards anchor

- **IIBA Business Analysis Competency Model** — the knowledge, skills, and
  characteristics behind effective BA work; take the competency dimensions and
  proficiency framing.
- **IIBA Business Analysis Standard** — the foundational view of modern practice;
  take the lifecycle and artifact framing.
- **Internal playbook** — the organization-specific layer that makes them usable.

Operationalize as the rules above; do not reproduce the standards' text.

---

## Quality bar — failure modes to catch

- Artifacts with no owner, approver, or rubric.
- Proficiency judged by knowledge tests alone.
- Gates with no written pass criteria.
- Approval authorities left undefined until sign-off is needed.
- Traceability links missing — an initiative that can't be walked from objective to
  benefit.
- Terms or artifacts in use that aren't in the catalogue or glossary.

---

## How to apply this skill

1. **Define** the lifecycle method and its quality gates.
2. **Build** the artifact catalogue with owners, approvers, and rubrics.
3. **Set** the competency matrix and evidence-based assessment.
4. **Assign** approval authorities.
5. **Stand up** the repository, naming, and version-control rules.
6. **Instantiate** the traceability model and run gate reviews against it.
